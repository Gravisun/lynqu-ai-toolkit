# Decision Makers — Northwind Brokerage

> Fictional example. See [README.md](README.md).

Northwind is a 400-agent regional brokerage. Two tracks: a corporate pilot, and
a bottom-up play with the region that already buys its own tools.

## Track A — Corporate pilot

All three have to say yes to the same purchase, so this is **one deal with three
participants**, not three leads.

| Person | Title | Role in the deal | Why them |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Dana Reyes | VP Agent Experience | **Champion / primary** | Owns agent tooling budget; has publicly complained about lead leakage at events |
| Marcus Oyelaran | CTO | Technical sponsor | Must bless anything touching the agent roster |
| Priya Shah | COO | Economic buyer | Signs above $25k; cares about time-to-productivity, not features |

**Entry point:** Dana. Marcus and Priya come in at the pilot-scoping call, not
before — leading with the CTO turns a workflow conversation into a security
review.

## Track B — Regional bottom-up

| Person | Title | Role in the deal | Why them |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Elena Kovač | Managing Broker, Lakeshore region | Sole decision maker | 60 agents, her own tools budget, no corporate approval needed |

Her own lead, her own track. A regional manager who can buy today should not
wait on a corporate pilot.

## Per-person first-line anchors

- **Dana** — the panel talk on "the 40% of event leads that never get entered"
- **Marcus** — Northwind's move to a single agent roster last year
- **Priya** — the stated goal of cutting new-agent ramp from 90 to 60 days
- **Elena** — Lakeshore's own event calendar; she runs four a year

## Flags

- Northwind renewed an incumbent CRM in January. Do not lead with replacement —
  lead with capture, which the incumbent does badly.
- Priya has never replied to a cold email. Route through Dana.
